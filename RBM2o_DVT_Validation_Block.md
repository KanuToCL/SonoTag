The RBM2o DVT validation effort is currently being severely hampered by persistent and systemic instabilities related to the debug clip and device communication stack, an issue which the lab team has deemed "unsustainable." Technically, the primary block is twofold: a mechanical/hardware flaw in the debug clips themselves and a cascading software/firmware communication failure. On the hardware front, the lab has experienced that the DVT clip pins are significantly "flakier than in previous P1 builds," which is suspected to be due to the logo and flat grip design, making pin alignment extremely hard to achieve. Crucially, the EE team has identified that the rim of the device-side connector is metal, meaning a slight misalignment can cause a short, potentially burning out the connector on the device itself, not just the debug clip (as seen on unit 004W). This poor mechanical tolerance occasionally results in a "SHORT_DETECTED" error. Furthermore, there is an alarming thermal/power issue where the clips sometimes draw "hecka power around 3 Wats and they get really hot," raising concerns that the overheating may be permanently breaking DVT devices. The software stability is equally critical; devices are becoming unresponsive, with Maui one-wire commands successfully putting the unit into fastboot, but the host machine subsequently failing to recognize the device for flashing or ADB communications. This inconsistent and unreliable connection forces the lab team to spend the majority of their time debugging devices entering a "panic state" rather than advancing critical audio testing tasks. Given this block, the issue has been escalated to key DRIs across the organization, including the PD/ME designer (Stanson Koh), EE architect (Steven Van Gent), and the USB Driver SW DRI (Matthew Heida), with Calvin Pan raising the issue in the RBM2 system meeting to ensure higher management is aware of the comprehensive and persistent testing block.

Suspected Hypotheses by the EE/PD Team (Updated)
A mechanical/hardware flaw is present in the debug clips themselves.
The logo and flat grip design of the clip are suspected to be making pin alignment extremely hard to achieve, which leads to a risk of shorting and burning out the device's connector due to the metal rim.
A cascading software/firmware communication failure is contributing to the instability.
The overheating caused by the high power draw (around 3 Wats) may be permanently breaking DVT devices.

Action Item (FW/SW Team)
The FW/SW team needs to investigate why, after a Maui unlock command, the MCU sometimes remains locked despite the system reporting it as unlocked. To successfully unlock the MCU, multiple Maui unlock commands are currently required.
